ACP, HPL and glass are the three facade materials Kerala building owners ask about most. ACP suits clean modern lines and a wide range of finishes. HPL suits realistic wood looks. Glass suits daylight and display. Most good facades combine two of them. Here is how they compare in Kerala's weather.

## The short version

| Feature | ACP | HPL | Glass |
|---|---|---|---|
| What it is | Aluminium skins on a core | Compressed resin and paper layers | Toughened or laminated glass |
| Best for | Modern lines, folds, signage | Realistic wood looks | Daylight, display, transparency |
| Weight | Light | Heavier | Heavy |
| Shapes | Folds, boxes, curves | Mostly flat | Flat |
| Heat | Reflects sun with an air gap | Similar, with an air gap | Lets heat in unless specified carefully |
| Upkeep | Wash once or twice a year | Wash, check edges | Frequent cleaning to look good |

## ACP: aluminium composite panel

ACP is two thin aluminium sheets bonded to a core. It can be routed and folded into trays, boxes, fins and bands, which makes it the most flexible of the three.

**In Kerala's weather.** Aluminium does not rust. Exterior-grade coatings resist UV. The weak points are the joints and the frame. Badly sealed joints let monsoon water behind the panels. Use an aluminium frame and stainless steel fixings.

**Fire.** Standard ACP has a combustible PE core. For taller and public buildings, specify a fire-retardant (FR) or A2-grade core.

**Choose ACP when** you want crisp modern lines, signage built into the facade, or a light system for an older building.

## HPL: high-pressure laminate

HPL panels are made by pressing layers of resin-soaked paper under heat. Exterior HPL has a weather-resistant surface.

**In Kerala's weather.** Exterior-grade HPL handles sun and rain well. The edges and fixing details need care in constant humidity.

**Look.** HPL's strength is realism. Wood-look HPL can be very convincing up close.

**Limits.** HPL is mostly used as flat panels. It does not fold like ACP, and it is heavier.

**Choose HPL when** a realistic timber look is the main goal, on flat surfaces.

## Glass

Glass facades bring daylight and let people see inside. For showrooms, that is often the whole point at street level.

**In Kerala's weather.** Glass itself does not mind rain. Heat does matter. Large areas of plain glass facing west can make interiors very hot. Performance glass and shading help, at a cost.

**Upkeep.** Glass shows dust and water marks. It needs frequent cleaning to look its best, especially near busy roads.

**Choose glass when** you need display and light: showroom fronts, lobbies and offices with a view.

## The common answer: combine them

Many of the best facades in Kerala use glass at ground level for display, and ACP above for shape, shade and signage. Wood-finish ACP or HPL adds warmth in small doses. One clear idea, two or three materials.

## What to ask before you choose

- Which way does the facade face, and how much afternoon sun does it get?
- Do you need to see in, or only to be seen?
- How much shape do you want: flat, or boxes and fins?
- How old is the structure, and how much weight can it take?
- Who will clean it, and how often?
