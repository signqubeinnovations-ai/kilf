A good shop front elevation is seen from the end of the street, read in a glance and makes walking in feel easy. These ten ideas work well on Kerala shops and showrooms. Most use ACP, because it folds, holds a clean line and carries signage. Pick two or three. Not all ten.

## 1. One big frame

Wrap the whole shop front in a single deep box frame. It turns a row of mismatched elements into one clear shape. It also shades the glass from the afternoon sun.

## 2. A signage band that belongs

Instead of a flex board on top, design a fascia band into the elevation. Letters sit in it at the right size and height. The building looks planned, and your name is easier to read.

## 3. Vertical fins for rhythm

Repeated fins along a wide front add depth and shadow. From a moving vehicle they create movement. They also hide AC units and pipes.

## 4. Warm wood above, glass below

Glass at street level for display, wood-finish ACP above for warmth. It feels premium without being cold. It suits textile, furniture and lifestyle stores.

## 5. Metallic for jewellery

Champagne, bronze and brushed finishes signal value. Pair them with a deep frame around the display and controlled lighting. Keep the palette tight. One metal, one dark tone.

## 6. A clear entrance

Customers should never wonder where to go in. Mark the entrance with a change in depth, colour or height. A small canopy also keeps monsoon rain off shoppers.

## 7. Your brand colour, at scale

If your brand has a strong colour, use it on one large, simple surface. A single bold plane is remembered from across the junction. Spread across ten small elements, it becomes noise.

## 8. Light it on purpose

Many shops look closed after dark. A slim LED line along the frame, wash light on the fascia and lit letters keep the shop visible at night. Plan the lighting in the design, so cables stay hidden.

## 9. Hide the clutter

AC units, cables, meters and old brackets make any front look untidy. A well-designed elevation screens them, or moves them, without blocking access for service.

## 10. Design the whole building, not just the shop

If the floors above are tired, a sharp new shop front can make them look worse. Even a simple treatment of the upper floors, such as one horizontal band, pulls the whole building together.

## How to choose

Start with three questions:

1. **Who passes your shop?** Walkers, two-wheelers or cars. That decides the size of letters and shapes.
2. **What do you sell?** Jewellery, groceries and furniture each call for a different mood.
3. **What do you need to show?** If customers must see products, glazing comes first.
