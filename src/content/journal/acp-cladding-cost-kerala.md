The cost of ACP cladding in Kerala is usually quoted per square foot, but no single rate fits every building. The price depends on the panel grade and finish, the framework, the design, the facade area and how easy the site is to reach. This guide explains each factor, so you can compare quotes properly.

## 1. Panel grade and core

ACP panels are two aluminium skins bonded to a core. The core changes the price.

- **Standard (PE) core.** The most common and most affordable. Combustible, so not right for every building.
- **Fire-retardant (FR) core.** Mineral-filled. Costs more. Recommended for taller and public buildings.
- **A2-grade core.** Higher mineral content and limited combustibility. The highest cost of the three.

Skin thickness and overall panel thickness also matter. Exterior facades need exterior-grade panels. Very cheap quotes sometimes use interior-grade panels outdoors. They fade and fail quickly in Kerala's sun and rain.

## 2. Coating and finish

The coating protects the colour. Exterior-grade coatings resist UV and weather far better than basic ones, and cost more.

The finish matters too:

- **Solid colours** are usually the most economical.
- **Metallics** cost more.
- **Wood and stone finishes** usually cost more again.

Most good facades combine one or two premium finishes with a solid colour. That keeps the look rich and the cost sensible.

## 3. The framework

ACP is fixed on a sub-frame. This is where quotes differ most, and where cheap work hides.

An aluminium framework does not rust and suits Kerala's humidity. Mild steel frames are cheaper but need proper protection, or they rust behind the panels. Frame spacing, anchors and brackets all add cost, and all affect how long the facade lasts.

Ask every contractor: what is the frame made of, and what are the fixings?

## 4. Design complexity

A flat wall of large panels is quick to make and fix. Every fold, box projection, fin, curve and cut-out adds fabrication and installation time. 3D features and custom patterns cost more per square foot than plain cladding.

This is not a reason to avoid design. A few well-placed features often do more than covering everything.

## 5. Facade area and wastage

Larger areas often bring a lower rate per square foot, because setup costs are shared. But panel sizes matter. Panels come in standard sheet sizes. A design that fits those sizes wastes less material. Good shop drawings reduce wastage and cost.

## 6. Site access and height

Scaffolding, lifting and working at height add cost. A ground-floor shop front on a quiet road is simpler than the top floors of a building on a busy junction. Work that must happen at night to keep a shop open can also add cost.

## 7. Signage and lighting

Letters, logo boxes, LED lines and illuminated signs are usually priced separately. Planning them into the facade from the start costs less than adding them later.

## 8. Old wall repairs

For renovation projects, the old wall may need repairs before cladding: loose plaster, leaks or damaged ledges. A good contractor finds these during the survey and tells you before quoting.

## How to compare two quotes

Put the quotes side by side and check:

| Ask about | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Panel brand, grade and core | Decides durability and fire performance |
| Coating type | Decides how long the colour lasts |
| Framework material and spacing | Decides strength and rust risk |
| Fixings | Stainless steel resists humidity |
| Joint and sealant detail | Keeps monsoon rain out |
| What is included | Design, drawings, signage, scaffolding, cleaning |

The lowest rate per square foot is not always the lowest cost. A facade that fades or leaks in three monsoons costs more in the end.
